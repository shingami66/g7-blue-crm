## 3. Meeting Context

The meeting should obtain one real event journey from customer inquiry through financial close, plus normal, changed, and failed-event examples. It should be a listening and evidence session, not a sales pitch or system demonstration.

Suggested opening:

> I am building an internal system for an events company. We already have customers, Services, quotations, invoices, payments, and a supplier foundation. I want to understand the real workflow used by event companies in Riyadh, especially suppliers, cost, operations, and financial close, so that we do not design the system from assumptions. I am not asking for confidential information; I want to understand the practical process and repeated problems.

Then ask:

> Could you walk me through one real event from the first customer request until the event was financially closed?

Use: Ask -> Listen -> Summarise -> Confirm -> Ask for a real example -> Move to the next stage.

## 4. Required Meeting Outcomes

Obtain, where possible:

1. One end-to-end event workflow.
2. One normal event, one major-change event, and one failure/emergency event.
3. Supplier quotation, selection, approval, delivery, acceptance, bill, and payment practice.
4. Venue, Site Visit, permit, insurance, labour, meal, transport, expense, and petty-cash practice.
5. Cost, margin, late-bill, and financial-close practice.
6. Role ownership for request, comparison, approval, receipt, review, payment, and close.
7. Repeated operational and financial problems.
8. A list of real documents and permission for anonymized samples where possible.

## 5. Highest-Priority Questions

Ask these first if time is limited:

1. Can you walk through one real event from the first request to financial close?
2. What document or message proves each major handoff?
3. How are suppliers found, compared, selected, approved, and paid?
4. When does a booking become a formal commitment?
5. How is supplier delivery accepted?
6. How are supplier failures, substitutions, deposits, credits, and late bills handled?
7. How are direct event costs separated from company overhead?
8. How are Expected Margin and Actual Margin calculated?
9. Who can request, approve, receive, review, pay, and close?
10. What single repeated problem would create the most value if solved?

## 6. Full Question Catalogue

Each group below states why its answers matter, the future module that depends on them, the decision blocked, preferred evidence, and the full source question set.

## 7. Client Inquiry and Event Lifecycle

**Why it matters:** Defines how Customer, Inquiry, Service, commercial work, and operations hand off.
**Future module:** CRM and Sales; Operations.
**Decision blocked:** Event stages, handoff ownership, and Event Brief timing.
**Preferred evidence:** One real timeline, messages, quotation, and approval record.

## 8. Event Brief

**Why it matters:** Distinguishes required event information from optional details.
**Future module:** Event Operations.
**Decision blocked:** Whether an Event Brief is mandatory and which fields block execution.
**Preferred evidence:** Completed brief or equivalent real checklist.

## 9. Venue Sourcing

**Why it matters:** Clarifies whether venue work belongs to the customer, G7 BLUE, or both.
**Future module:** Operations and Commercial.
**Decision blocked:** Venue responsibility, comparison, deposit, and approval flow.
**Preferred evidence:** Venue shortlist, quote, contract, or approval message.

## 10. Site Visit

**Why it matters:** Identifies technical, safety, access, and venue-condition checks.
**Future module:** Operations, Risks, Documents.
**Decision blocked:** When Site Visit is mandatory and what outcome states exist.
**Preferred evidence:** Site Visit checklist, photos, risk list, and follow-up.

## 11. Supplier Discovery

**Why it matters:** Shows how the supplier master is actually created and maintained.
**Future module:** Suppliers and Procurement.
**Decision blocked:** Supplier qualification, preferred status, coverage, and retirement rules.
**Preferred evidence:** Supplier list, onboarding form, and performance notes.

## 12. Supplier Qualification

**Why it matters:** Separates supplier preference from licence, insurance, capability, and compliance evidence.
**Future module:** Suppliers, Documents, Risks.
**Decision blocked:** Required supplier documents and approval statuses.
**Preferred evidence:** Anonymized supplier profile and current document examples.

## 13. RFQ Practice

**Why it matters:** Determines whether formal RFQ is normal or only needed in selected cases.
**Future module:** Procurement.
**Decision blocked:** RFQ thresholds, channels, scope templates, and emergency exceptions.
**Preferred evidence:** RFQ email, message, spreadsheet, or equivalent.

## 14. Supplier Quotations

**Why it matters:** Reveals real quote formats, terms, scope differences, and data-entry burden.
**Future module:** Supplier Quotations and Document Capture.
**Decision blocked:** Quote fields, attachment handling, and AI/OCR priority.
**Preferred evidence:** PDF, image, Excel, email, or message quote with permission.

## 15. Quote Comparison

**Why it matters:** The cheapest price may not be lowest total risk or cost.
**Future module:** Quote Comparison and Reports.
**Decision blocked:** Manual comparison, scoring, scope normalization, and required reason.
**Preferred evidence:** Real comparison sheet and selected/rejected rationale.

## 16. Supplier Selection and Approval

**Why it matters:** Identifies who makes the commercial and operational decision.
**Future module:** Procurement and Permissions.
**Decision blocked:** Approval roles, limits, and separation of duties.
**Preferred evidence:** Approval message, signed decision, or workflow record.

## 17. Purchase Orders and Contracts

**Why it matters:** Establishes the natural commitment point and amendment history.
**Future module:** Purchase Orders and Supplier Accounting.
**Decision blocked:** PO use, approval thresholds, contract relationship, and changes.
**Preferred evidence:** Purchase Order, contract, amendment, or supplier acceptance.

## 18. Supplier Booking

**Why it matters:** Preserves the current distinction between operational reservation and financial commitment.
**Future module:** Supplier Booking alignment and Procurement.
**Decision blocked:** How existing bookings transition to formal procurement, if at all.
**Preferred evidence:** Booking confirmation and later PO or contract, if both exist.

## 19. Payment Terms and Credit

**Why it matters:** Determines payable ageing, advances, credit limits, and cash planning.
**Future module:** Supplier Payments and Accounts Payable.
**Decision blocked:** Standard terms, approval, and credit controls.
**Preferred evidence:** Supplier quote, contract, invoice, and payment record.

## 20. Supplier Advances

**Why it matters:** Advances are settlement against later payable unless terms say otherwise.
**Future module:** Advances and Supplier Payments.
**Decision blocked:** Advance evidence, allocation, and treatment on cancellation.
**Preferred evidence:** Advance request, receipt, transfer, and final invoice.

## 21. Refundable Deposits

**Why it matters:** Refundable security is not automatically expense.
**Future module:** Deposits, Claims, and Cash.
**Decision blocked:** Return timing, custodian, damage retention, and follow-up alerts.
**Preferred evidence:** Deposit receipt, return proof, incident, or settlement.

## 22. Service Delivery and Acceptance

**Why it matters:** A Service Receipt is the evidence bridge between approved scope and Vendor Bill.
**Future module:** Service Receipts and three-way review.
**Decision blocked:** Acceptance fields, conditional acceptance, and authority.
**Preferred evidence:** Checklist, signature, photos, delivery message, or acceptance email.

## 23. Supplier Failure and Substitution

**Why it matters:** Event-day failures require controlled changes and financial recovery.
**Future module:** Procurement exceptions, Incidents, Credits, Claims.
**Decision blocked:** Substitution authority, price difference, deductions, credit, and blacklist.
**Preferred evidence:** Failure report, replacement quote, credit note, or incident record.

## 24. Vendor Bills

**Why it matters:** Bills may arrive late, differ from commitment, or lack delivery evidence.
**Future module:** Vendor Bills and Accounts Payable.
**Decision blocked:** Review, matching, variance, duplicate, and close behavior.
**Preferred evidence:** Vendor Bill, PO, receipt, and approval record.

## 25. Supplier Credits

**Why it matters:** Credits recover value from overpayment, cancelled scope, or service failure.
**Future module:** Supplier Credits and Payables.
**Decision blocked:** Credit creation, allocation, expiry, and refund treatment.
**Preferred evidence:** Credit note, settlement, or supplier correspondence.

## 26. Supplier Payments

**Why it matters:** Payment is settlement, not the birth of cost.
**Future module:** Supplier Payments and Bank/Cash.
**Decision blocked:** Payment approval, allocation, evidence, and reconciliation.
**Preferred evidence:** Bank transfer, receipt, payment batch, or remittance.

## 27. Company Expenses

**Why it matters:** Overhead must remain separate from direct event costs.
**Future module:** Company Expenses and Company P&L.
**Decision blocked:** Categories, approval thresholds, recurring expenses, and budgets.
**Preferred evidence:** Expense register, invoices, approvals, and budget examples.

## 28. Employee Reimbursements

**Why it matters:** Employees may spend personal money for event work and need evidence-based reimbursement.
**Future module:** Expenses and Reimbursements.
**Decision blocked:** Claimant eligibility, receipt exceptions, review, approval, and payment.
**Preferred evidence:** Anonymized claim and receipt.

## 29. Petty Cash

**Why it matters:** Small urgent spending needs accountable custody and settlement.
**Future module:** Petty Cash and Cash Control.
**Decision blocked:** Custodians, top-ups, settlement frequency, shortage handling.
**Preferred evidence:** Petty-cash book, settlement, receipts, or cash count.

## 30. Daily Labour

**Why it matters:** Labour may be supplier-provided or direct and may have HR implications.
**Future module:** Crew, Labour, Expenses, HR boundary.
**Decision blocked:** Attendance, rates, approvals, payment evidence, and legal-status controls.
**Preferred evidence:** Attendance sheet, supplier bill, or approved labour record.

## 31. Crew Meals and Transport

**Why it matters:** Meals and transport caused by an event are direct event costs.
**Future module:** Event Costing and Operations.
**Decision blocked:** Responsibility, approval, evidence, and Service linkage.
**Preferred evidence:** Event plan, receipts, supplier scope, or settlement.

## 32. Insurance

**Why it matters:** Policy, premium, event coverage, and expiry have different meanings.
**Future module:** Documents, Risks, Expenses.
**Decision blocked:** Annual versus event-specific treatment and reminders.
**Preferred evidence:** Policy schedule, certificate, premium invoice, or venue requirement.

## 33. Damage and Incidents

**Why it matters:** Incident facts, claims, deposits, and settlements must not be conflated.
**Future module:** Risks, Claims, Deposits, and Operations.
**Decision blocked:** Responsibility, evidence, approval, and financial settlement.
**Preferred evidence:** Incident Report, photos, claim, and settlement.

## 34. Permits

**Why it matters:** Missing permits may block execution and have responsible parties.
**Future module:** Documents and Operations.
**Decision blocked:** Required document types, owner, expiry, and blocking status.
**Preferred evidence:** Permit checklist, approval, expiry, and attachment.

## 35. Event Costing

**Why it matters:** Event cost needs one language for estimates, commitments, actuals, paid, and outstanding.
**Future module:** Event Cost Ledger.
**Decision blocked:** Sources, categories, credit treatment, contingency, and close.
**Preferred evidence:** Cost sheet, supplier ledger, or event close report.

## 36. Event Margin

**Why it matters:** Expected and actual margin depend on revenue and cost policy.
**Future module:** Profitability and Reports.
**Decision blocked:** Revenue basis, overhead allocation, and statutory versus managerial labels.
**Preferred evidence:** Real profitability report and accountant review.

## 37. Event Financial Close

**Why it matters:** Close determines when late bills, deposits, credits, and claims are resolved.
**Future module:** Costing, Supplier Finance, Accounting Periods.
**Decision blocked:** Close authority, late-bill reopening, and evidence completeness.
**Preferred evidence:** Closed event file and late-bill example.

## 38. Company Accounting

**Why it matters:** Expenses and supplier finance must not create duplicate accounting truth.
**Future module:** Chart of Accounts, Journals, AR/AP, Bank, Statements.
**Decision blocked:** Accounting engine sequence and professional policy.
**Preferred evidence:** Existing accountant-approved chart, journals, and close process.

## 39. Reports

**Why it matters:** Management reports must use consistent source values and clear definitions.
**Future module:** Reports Center.
**Decision blocked:** Required reports, audience, frequency, and drill-down.
**Preferred evidence:** Repeated management requests and current spreadsheets.

## 40. Dashboards

**Why it matters:** Each role needs useful work queues without exposing sensitive values.
**Future module:** Role-Based Dashboards.
**Decision blocked:** Roles, widgets, scopes, and mobile needs.
**Preferred evidence:** Daily role routines and current dashboard requests.

## 41. Roles and Permissions

**Future module:** Permission Matrix and Approval Rules.
**Decision blocked:** Role versus assignment scope and separation of duties.
